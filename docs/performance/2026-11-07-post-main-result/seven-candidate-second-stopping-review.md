## Remaining small source work: real, but not a supported next experiment

**Transient split-row counting remains the smallest unmeasured cleanup.** At graph/root_structure.rs:181–183, the builder uses split_rows.len() for an encounter ordinal and pushes a row. finish_directory at :210–220 clears and rebuilds the same Vec in ascending identity order. A scalar can preserve the encounter ordinal while deleting the first sequence of writes.

That is a distinct unmeasured treatment; the pending-reserve rejection did not test it. However, the final split directory, its storage, final population, ordered-row sort, shared payloads and full traversal remain required. In a zero-hint path, allocation can move to final population rather than disappear. The current timed directory frames locate necessary final work, not the first discarded write sequence. The safe direct saving is short usize stores and Vec bookkeeping during structural rebuild, not a removed result buffer or an avoided full scan. Outcome ordinals must also remain independent of sorted iterator order. This does not currently have a sufficient complete-benefit case to justify another performance screen merely because the patch is easy.

**Ready-root finish still contains constant reads, not a hidden O(N) installation.** canonical.rs:231–339 creates empty local containers and checks final source/frontier/placement. For a nonempty-source center/gap transfer, the bodies remain Ready, equality avoids placement COW, and complete_item_move_structure returns without rebuilding rows. Empty Vec/SecondaryMap headers are not nonempty heap allocations. A narrow early return can remove some lookups but not another large traversal.

A larger shortcut must preserve empty-source removal, central retention, retired presentation cleanup, inserted-split/outer-root normalization, stable independent-root error order, and final live-frontier weight diagnostics. The real edge-edit row build supplies fresh immutable directory joins for owned output; avoiding it requires a separate incremental structural design. Neither source nor the current samples establishes a new large duplicate completion to delete. This early-return idea is unmeasured, not disproven by source-nonempty; its smaller direct saving and branch/error obligations make it a weak next experiment.

**Extending recapture borrowing to edges would be a variant of the measured mechanism.** The current edge path does reverify and recapture, as the source-bound timed sample shows. Live Tabs facts failing does not logically prove that edge borrowing would fail. But the remaining direct deletion is again repeated membership derivation and one temporary RootReference retain/drop, without eliminating normative root issuance or an output allocation. Inner targets must preserve selected/rule facts; outer targets must still derive the actual root node, and scope-before-reference/error ordering remains. There is no new edge-specific causal observation supporting a materially larger deletion than the tested Tabs treatment. I would not iterate through adjacent branches solely because one remains unmeasured.

**Single-item policy inventory has a real owned temporary, with a broader evaluator seam.** operation.rs:1363–1389 creates one-item policy facts before mutation. DockPayloadPolicyFacts at policy.rs:1026 owns a Vec and supports public/serde inputs; its existing singleton duplicate check is already skipped after the empty check at :2083–2095. The actual outcome item Vec is separate, required owned report data, not a second redundant policy inventory.

A private borrowed payload view could avoid constructing that temporary for the single Item path, but preserving one evaluator means threading the view through source, surface/target, operation/class, central and duplicate rules. It must retain error ordering and arbitrary owned/serde facts; copying a default-policy subset into a second fast evaluator would be wrong. Current evidence does not isolate this inventory allocation as a complete-operation bottleneck. This is not an impossible design or an experimentally disproven deletion. It is a larger ownership/API change than a hidden one-line reserve fix, without enough current benefit evidence to require it now.

The reasoning above is not that each candidate must independently close the entire 2x gap. It is that these residuals lack a strong enough individual complete-benefit/risk case. The previous tests also demonstrate why source-level work deletion alone is insufficient for retention; they do not prove a universal unfavorable compiler effect.

## Compiled events, scratch, and rich owned results

The primary sixteen-pane fixtures are **binary trees**, not sixteen-way splits. docking_cost.rs:72–80 always constructs two children. With one view, sixteen leaves have 31 rows; with four views, four four-leaf trees have seven rows each, 28 total. The edge operation introduces a binary wrapper or a local same-axis result; pane count alone is not evidence of n-ary scratch demand.

Binary constraints, converted weights and sizes already use local arrays in layout.rs:1066–1103 and :1127–1137. ProjectionScratch begins with empty Vec headers, and the eligible Vec is used by actual redistribution. The root projection stack already has the retained small initial reserve. “Reuse sixteen-child scratch” therefore targets the wrong ordinary shape.

Cross-root scratch reuse would need a new caller-owned batch seam, hidden persistent scratch, or movement of scratch through multiple public project_root calls. The frozen primary caller at docking_cost.rs:215–256 invokes project_root independently for each root; changing it to exploit a new scene API changes the probe. A hidden workspace/thread-local owner introduces reentrancy, concurrency, retention and failed-call lifetime obligations. Scene-only reuse may help another independently measured workload, but the current matrix does not establish that opportunity. It must not reuse prior constraints or output values.

Precompiled geometry events could reduce continuation tag/push work, but would add or derive another event schedule, retain it with immutable topology, and pay construction/storage costs when an edge changes the root. They must still solve the parent before child bounds, preserve the old splitter thickness, finish descendants before constructing the following gap, keep numeric/error ordering, and allow checked raw-DAG repeats with last-write geometry. The raw and production scheduling seam is substantial; a second solver/executor or a new unbounded metadata cache would worsen the design risk.

The current samples locate the enclosing continuation/projection work, not a measured net benefit after event production and changed-root costs. The failed prefix treatment removed a genuinely per-Enter operation with a tiny patch; compiled events are a different, unmeasured architecture, not a required “next small step.” I do not infer that their failure is certain, only that there is not yet a supported benefit/cost case for implementing them under this task.

Fresh rich output is a retained contract, not spare work available for deletion. NodeMap owns every fresh NodeResult; SplitMap owns complete outcomes and per-edge child values. Results and clones outlive workspaces and independent candidates. The formal export linearly joins owned geometry with current graph identity/items/selection into a fresh complete word vector. None can become a lazy facade, previous-frame cache, skipped leaf read, or partial central-only output.

Likewise, independent candidate creation, exact root issuance after a real change, MRU permutations, and atomic rollback cannot be eliminated by temporarily mutating the original and undoing it. Persistent NodeStore and rejected numerical variants need genuinely different evidence before reopening. This report supplies no new container, cache, dependency, or solver proposal.

## Stopping recommendation and limits

I support stopping runtime implementation now because the concrete newly located deletions were resolved by actual experiments, including the two residuals that prevented the preceding unconditional stop, and the remaining source work is either smaller without distinct cost evidence or requires a substantially wider ownership/scheduling change. **The reason is not the count seven.**

The precise statement is: under the restored c7508ee/0e441c8 source, unchanged complete metric, and preserved owned/error/reference contracts, this continuation currently has no further implementation with a sufficient supported payoff. No runtime winner was retained. The result remains 30/42 with twelve small misses. This is not theoretical optimality, an allocator/compiler lower bound, a proof that 42/42 cannot be reached, or a causal decomposition of elapsed time. Inclusive samples must not be converted to component shares or summed into a lower bound.

A future reopening should identify a different concrete expensive owner/path, a substantive measured source of duplicated complete work, or a separately authorized workload/architectural objective. A new idea alone, another capacity sweep, combining rejected patches without a reasoned interaction, or selecting the favorable part of an old campaign is not such evidence.

The release prefix guard is restored and should remain in this retained state. The explicit internal-diagnostic scope relaxation was authorized and tested; its rejection neither proves that valid producers need the guard nor justifies quietly keeping its weaker release behavior without the measured benefit.

This review does not mark the overall goal complete. Root still needs to incorporate the final accurate evidence/review/restoration records, publish the intended final head, and verify **that exact head's six CI jobs**. Prior main CI does not certify a later head. I made no network request and do not certify final publication or remote status.
